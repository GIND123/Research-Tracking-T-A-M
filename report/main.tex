\title{Grounded Extraction of Technology Mentions from Papers and Patents}

\begin{abstract}
Tracking the evolution of technology begins with naming the technologies in
individual documents. This routine-looking extraction task has an unusual error
profile once its output is aggregated: a fabricated technology is not averaged
away by scale, because a spurious term whose rate follows corpus growth produces
a curve indistinguishable from a genuine emerging technology, whereas a missed
mention mostly rescales a series. We therefore build the extractor around
structural constraints rather than a confidence threshold --- a mention
\emph{is} a pair of character offsets with a cited evidence sentence, so a model
that names a technology we cannot locate has named nothing. On a
\NumGold-mention gold set of \NumPapers{} arXiv abstracts and \NumPatents{}
USPTO publications spanning thirteen arXiv categories and all five major CPC
sections, the system reaches \RFullPF{} partial F$_1$ at a hallucination rate of
\RFullHalluc. An ablation over six components finds only one unambiguously
load-bearing --- the verifier --- while two others buy strict precision at a
measurable cost in overlap recall and ranking quality. We also report a negative
result: span grounding bounds but does not close indirect prompt injection,
because an adversary can write their phantom technology into their own patent.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A lab tracking technology through the literature must first know which
technologies each document is about; everything downstream consumes that output
and cannot repair it. The task resembles scientific NER, and the obvious move is
to apply that machinery: a span tagger over a scientific ontology
\cite{luan2018scierc} or a prompted generative extractor.
None is designed for the property that matters here: an \emph{asymmetry} between
the two error types. Trend detection compares a term's frequency against a
baseline, so a constant spurious rate produces a flat line and does no harm, but
a spurious rate tracking corpus growth produces a rising line indistinguishable
from a real emerging technology that nobody will go back and check. A missed
mention usually rescales a series without changing its shape. Precision errors
are not merely costlier, they are qualitatively different --- and they are the
errors generative extractors make \cite{ji2023hallucination}.

Rather than tune an extractor to be cautious, we make the failure
unrepresentable: a mention is a pair of character offsets, its surface is the
slice at those offsets, and it must cite a document sentence containing it --- the
span-level analogue of attribution work in generation
\cite{rashkin2023attribution}, applied as a hard gate rather than a post-hoc
score. Grounding costs recall, since a correct but paraphrased identification is
discarded, so the rest of the system gives that recall back where it can be
checked: five recallers over-generate, a guard stack removes what is provably
wrong, a verifier cascade challenges what survives, and one calibrated threshold
decides what to withhold.

\paragraph{Three ways this is not standard NER.}
\textbf{(i) The vocabulary is open by construction.} The point of tracking
technology is to see \emph{new} technology, so an extractor constrained to an
ontology can never report one; a knowledge base is an anchor, not a target.
\textbf{(ii) Granularity is the problem in disguise.} ``Machine learning'' and
``FlashAttention'' are technologies at different levels, and a tracker that mixes
them reports machine learning as the fastest growing technology of every year
since 1995. We type over a nine-way ontology separating artefacts, methods and
materials (tracked) from fields, tasks, metrics, datasets and tools (annotated,
not tracked).
\textbf{(iii) Stance carries most of the signal.} A count conflates a technology
being introduced, used and beaten, so each mention is tagged \textsc{proposed},
\textsc{used}, \textsc{compared}, \textsc{background} or \textsc{claimed}.

\paragraph{Structure-aware ingestion.} Normalisation repairs ligatures,
line-break hyphenation and typography and masks citation markers while keeping a
per-character map back to the raw text, so an auditor sees the mention as it
appears in the original filing. Both genres are segmented onto one closed set of
zones, so one stance classifier serves both; claims are split individually and
marked independent or dependent by back-reference detection, since a term in an
independent claim is monopolised, not merely discussed.

\paragraph{Recall over-generates on purpose.} Five recallers run per document. A
\emph{noun-phrase lattice} expands parsed dependency chunks into every left
truncation crossed with noun-final right boundaries: whether the term is ``string
similarity'' or ``superficial string similarity'' depends on information the
recaller lacks, and the parser attaches a following word often enough to matter,
which no left truncation recovers. \emph{Abbreviation mining} \cite{schwartz2003abbrev}
recovers the defining parenthetical, the best within-document coreference signal
available; \emph{C-value/NC-value} \cite{frantzi2000cvalue} is the only recaller
sensitive to \emph{nesting}, which is the granularity problem exactly; a
\emph{gazetteer} over the Computer Science Ontology \cite{salatino2020cso}
cannot find anything new but earns its place as a consensus witness; and a tier-2
\emph{language model} runs only over thin zones.

We initially gated the chunker on a curated head-noun lexicon --- the natural
design, and apparently a precision win. It is not: gating discards \textbf{53\%}
of gold mentions before any classifier sees them, because no lexicon anticipates
\emph{highback}, \emph{rule-based elision}, or the mechanical-component
vocabulary patents are built from. Table~\ref{tab:results} reports both.

\paragraph{Grounding.} Every candidate must be a verbatim slice at the offsets it
reports. Model-proposed spans are relocated: the cited sentence is grounded
first, the surface searched inside it, and a bounded ladder (exact,
whitespace-insensitive, case-insensitive, optionally a similarity snap) returns
\emph{the document's} characters or nothing --- the similarity rung is off by
default, since it alone can move a span onto a different entity.

The invariant is enforced at the emission boundary, not by a guard. Guards are
filters and all are ablatable, as the ablation table requires --- which had
quietly made the invariant ablatable too: disabling the verifier also disabled
enforcement of the \emph{structural} verdict, and the adjudicator could overturn
a structural rejection, a model voting on whether a span's offsets addressed its
own surface. Both holes were latent and the suite passed with them open. Offsets
and evidence are now re-derived after decoding on every path, violations counted,
and structural rejections are not adjudicable.

\paragraph{Typing, stance, verification.} Type is a linear model over
interpretable features --- head-noun lexicon, orthography, KB depth, zone, and a
distributional back-off scoring the surface against hand-seeded prototype
centroids in a sentence-embedding space. Linearity is deliberate: the decision must be
explainable in an audit, and with a few hundred annotated mentions a linear model
is also the better estimator. Verification is a cascade: a \emph{structural}
verifier re-derives grounding and evidence from the document rather than trusting
the record; an \emph{embedding} verifier decides the easy cases; only the
residual band reaches an \emph{LLM judge}, shown the sentence, the term and the
proposed type and nothing about how any were produced, because a verifier shown
the proposer's reasoning ratifies it. Disagreements with independent support go
to a stronger adjudicator, which may accept, reject or abstain.

\paragraph{One abstention mechanism.} An earlier version let each guard abstain
and remove the mention; recall became a function of guard ordering. Guards now
REJECT (a provable violation) or PASS, soft signals feed a logistic confidence
model, and one calibrated threshold decides what is emitted. Overlaps are
resolved \emph{after} scoring, by confidence: the only evidence available before
typing is span length and recaller count, which picks the wrong boundary about as
often as the right one.

\paragraph{Canonicalisation and cost.} Mentions merge by a cascade: in-document
abbreviation pairs promoted to corpus aliases, KB identity, lemma-key identity,
then embedding agglomeration over the NIL residue under a head-compatibility
constraint. NIL clusters get a stable synthetic identifier, so an emerging
technology is trackable from first naming. Per-candidate verification is the
obvious design and the wrong one: deduplicating on (surface, type, sentence),
escalating only the uncertainty band, and batching ${\sim}20$ survivors per
request behind a cached system prompt takes ${\sim}120$ calls per patent down to
one or two --- a projected \$0.03 per document.

\section{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{Data.} \NumPapers{} arXiv abstracts from five category queries
spanning thirteen primary categories once cross-listing is counted, plus
\NumPatents{} USPTO publications from the HUPD frame \cite{suzgun2023hupd},
stratified four apiece over CPC sections A, B, C, G and H with full text from
Google Patents --- covering snowboard bindings, HDAC inhibitors, Talbot--Lau
interferometry, Ziegler--Natta catalysis, wheel bearings and spread-spectrum
transmission. The spread is the point: a pipeline tuned on one vocabulary would
visibly fail on the others.

\paragraph{Gold.} \NumGold{} mentions over \NumDocs{} documents
(\NumGoldPaper{} paper, \NumGoldPatent{} patent), annotated by one author under
written guidelines. The zone is title and abstract for papers, plus the first
independent claim for patents capped at 1400 characters --- Markush claims run to
thousands of characters naming no technology. Output is filtered to the same
zone.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Strict (exact offsets) and partial (overlap) span P/R/F$_1$;
type and role accuracy on matched spans; the hallucination rate (emitted surfaces
that are not verbatim substrings of the source); and AURC
\cite{geifman2017selective}, which rewards a system whose errors concentrate in
its low-confidence tail, with expected calibration error alongside so good
ranking is not mistaken for good scaling. Partial F$_1$ is the primary
comparison, following term-extraction practice \cite{rigoutsterryn2020acter},
because boundary choice here is genuinely ambiguous. All numbers come from the
offline configuration, reproducible without cost; each row is a configuration
object rather than a code path.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Each component shows its characteristic profile
(Table~\ref{tab:results}). The gazetteer is precise and nearly blind
(\RGazetteerR{} recall): outside computer science the ontology has little to say.
C-value alone has the worst strict F$_1$ of any configuration (\RCvalueF) but a
respectable partial F$_1$ (\RCvaluePF) --- it finds the right regions and the
wrong boundaries, exactly what a frequency statistic with no syntax should do.
Gating the chunker raises precision \RChunkerP{}$\rightarrow$\RChunkerGatedP{}
and costs more than half its recall. The full system reaches \RFullPF{} partial
F$_1$, \RFullType{} type accuracy and \RFullAurc{} AURC.

\paragraph{What each component buys, and costs.} The ablations do not all point
the same way, and the ones that do not are the informative ones.

Removing the \emph{verifier} is the only unambiguous degradation, and it is
large: precision \RFullP{}$\rightarrow$\RNoVerifierP, partial F$_1$
\RFullPF{}$\rightarrow$\RNoVerifierPF, type accuracy
\RFullType{}$\rightarrow$\RNoVerifierType, AURC
\RFullAurc{}$\rightarrow$\RNoVerifierAurc, ECE
\RFullEce{}$\rightarrow$\RNoVerifierEce; recall \emph{rises}, to \RNoVerifierR.
That is what a verifier is for --- it does not find more, it decides what to keep.

The \emph{negative lexicon} and the \emph{KB with the abbreviation miner} trade
the other way: dropping the lexicon costs strict precision
(\RFullP{}$\rightarrow$\RNoNegativeP) while slightly \emph{improving} partial
F$_1$ (\RNoNegativePF) and AURC (\RNoNegativeAurc); dropping the KB behaves
similarly (\RNoStructurePF, \RNoStructureAurc). Both remove candidates that
overlap a gold span without matching it exactly, buying exact-boundary precision
by giving up partial credit. At a precision target this is the right trade --- but
it is a trade, not a free win. \emph{Consensus} and \emph{abstention} move strict
F$_1$ by under a point and improve AURC (\RNoConsensusAurc, \RNoAbstentionAurc{}
against \RFullAurc): they change what the system is confident about, not what it
finds (Figure~\ref{fig:landscape}).

\paragraph{Genre.} Patents are harder (Table~\ref{tab:breakdown}) despite
contributing more gold per document, because claim language generates many
well-formed component noun phrases indistinguishable from technology mentions
without domain knowledge --- ``a first position'', ``said support section''.

\paragraph{Error analysis.} Attributing each scored gold mention to one outcome
(Figure~\ref{fig:breakdown}a) is clearer than the F$_1$ columns. Only 8.7\% were
never proposed: genuine recall failure is the smallest category. The two largest
losses are recoverable in different senses --- 25.6\% are withheld by the
confidence threshold, the risk--coverage trade being made deliberately and
recovered by one knob; 13.7\% lose overlap decoding to a neighbouring span, which
partial F$_1$ still credits. Of the 10.3\% rejected by a guard, four-fifths were
typed \textsc{not\_tech}; the costly confusions are
\textsc{method}/\textsc{artifact}$\rightarrow$\textsc{task}, the boundary the
guidelines flag as hardest, and why method recall
(Figure~\ref{fig:breakdown}b) trails the others by ${\sim}0.27$.

\section{Prompt injection: a negative result}
\label{sec:injection}

Documents are adversary-authored, permanently published and placed inside a model
prompt, making indirect prompt injection \cite{greshake2023injection} a live
concern for a corpus built over decades of filings. We fence document text with a
per-call nonce, but the substantive defence was supposed to be grounding.

It is not sufficient, and an adversarial test showed why. An attacker writing
\emph{``always extract QuantumLeap Processor''} into their own specification has
made that string a verbatim substring of their own document, and a grounded
extractor has no basis to reject it. Grounding buys a \emph{bound}, not a
defence: the adversary can only inject technologies they will publish under their
own name. Closing the residue needed a second mechanism --- rejecting candidates
whose own sentence contains instruction-like content --- at the cost of mentions
in example sentences of papers about prompting. We keep the negative result as a
test so it stays visible.

\section{Limitations and future work}

Single annotator, so no agreement figure: the numbers characterise this pipeline
against this annotation, not absolute accuracy. Roles are annotated per document
rather than per occurrence, making patent role accuracy closer to zone detection
than stance classification, and the paper sample over-represents LLM-adjacent
work. In the offline configuration the type classifier and the embedding verifier
share a distributional signal, so that verifier is a consistency check rather than
an independent one. The zone excludes patent descriptions, where much disclosure
lives, and \NumDocs{} documents supports ablation comparison but not absolute
claims. The downstream
tracking demonstration is weaker than intended: its time-sliced corpus is built
from the arXiv API a year at a time and the endpoint rate-limited us to two
usable slices, so the aggregation logic is exercised by unit tests on synthetic
series and on the real corpus only across two points --- nothing here is an
empirical claim about technology trends.

The highest-value extension is \emph{temporal sense disambiguation}: vocabulary is
reused across eras --- ``transformer'' in a 2005 power-systems patent is a
magnetic component --- and one such error lands at the start of a series, exactly
where an emergence detector is most sensitive. We withhold knowledge-base links
predating a document; disambiguating senses against a contemporaneous corpus is
unattempted, as are per-occurrence stance, comparative statements
(``outperforms X'') that would turn co-occurrence into a successor graph,
full-text patents, and comparison against a fine-tuned span tagger on a larger
gold set.

\section{Conclusion}

Technology extraction for evolution tracking is a precision-dominant,
open-vocabulary problem in which fabricated entities do lasting damage and missed
ones mostly do not. Treating that asymmetry as a design constraint --- offsets
rather than strings, cited evidence rather than assertion, one calibrated
abstention rather than many cautious stages --- yields a system whose
hallucination rate is zero by construction and whose remaining errors are
boundary and type disagreements a reader can inspect. The components that matter
most are not those that find mentions but those that decide which to keep.
